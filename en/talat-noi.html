Talat Noi in Half a Day — Shrine, Hokkien Mansion, Church and Spare-Parts Lanes, One ฿5 Ferry from Us

⛴️ ฿5 ferry + ~12 min on foot 🕒 08:30–12:30 · ~2.5 km of walking 🏮 Nothing to pay, except at So Heng Tai 🛥️ Back by the same ferry or via ICONSIAM
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons

Talat Noi is the old Chinese quarter just south of Song Wat Road, pressed against the river and only a kilometre from Ratchawong Pier, where our ฿5 ferry lands. Most guests have never heard of it, because it has no main road and no neon: just two-metre lanes where second-hand engine shops, a Hokkien shrine older than two centuries, a courtyard mansion, a spired Catholic church and warehouse cafés sit within ten minutes of each other. We send guests in the morning, when the lanes are still in shade, and the walk ends at River City right on lunchtime.

Why Talat Noi, and not only Yaowarat

Yaowarat is a street for eating; Talat Noi is a set of lanes for working. This is where the Thai word “siang kong”, now used for every second-hand car-parts market in the country, comes from: the riverside Chow Sue Kong Shrine, and the parts shops around it that have passed through three or four generations. Old engines stacked outside shophouses are a sight no other Bangkok neighbourhood has. What makes it right for a half-day is that nothing needs a vehicle: shrine to church is under ten minutes on foot, church to River City another five, and River City has toilets, air-conditioning and a pier with two ways home. If you have already done Yaowarat at night, this is the daytime face of the same district, several times quieter.

The route — from the pier at the end of our lane to River City

  1. 08:30 — Ferry from Tha Din Daeng to Ratchawong, then Song Wat Road (~15 min) Board at the pier at the end of Soi Tha Din Daeng 16, pay ฿5, and you are at Ratchawong in 3 minutes. Turn right out of the pier onto Song Wat Road and follow the river south-east for about a kilometre, past old shophouses and rice warehouses turned into cafés. Most of them are still shut at this hour, which makes it an easy, shaded walk. When the road narrows and engines start appearing on the pavement, you are in Talat Noi.
  2. 08:50 — Chow Sue Kong Shrine: the riverside Hokkien shrine the district is named after (~20 min) Built by Hokkien settlers in the early Rattanakosin period, the shrine faces the river across a stone forecourt where shopkeepers light incense before opening. The curved tiled roof, the dragons along the ridge and much of the carved woodwork inside are original. Entry is free. Stand on the forecourt and look across the water and you see the Khlong San bank where we are; it is obvious at once why the first Chinese traders chose this spot, since a junk could tie up right in front.
  3. 09:10 — Soi Wanit 2 and the Rong Kueak Shrine lane: spare parts and street art (~40 min) From the shrine, walk into Soi Wanit 2, the district's main lane. Both sides are second-hand parts shops that have been here since the grandparents' generation, with engines, axles and wheels lined up until they spill onto the road. Between them are murals that artists add to every year, and an old classic car parked so long it has become the neighbourhood's photo spot. A side turning leads to Rong Kueak Shrine, a small Chinese shrine in a narrow alley named after the shoe workshops that once stood here. Go slowly: the real thing here is the shops still at work, not the pretty walls.
  4. 09:50 — So Heng Tai Mansion: a Hokkien courtyard house more than 200 years old (~30 min) Hidden down a side lane, this is a Chinese merchant family's house built in the Hokkien manner, four wings around a central court, with wooden gates, Chinese signboards and a tiled roof of a kind only a handful of Bangkok houses still have. The descendants still live here and open it to visitors. The courtyard has been turned into a pool used for diving lessons, which sounds odd but is what has kept the house standing. There is an entry charge that includes a drink, and the opening hours are not fixed; if the gate is shut, carry on and come back on your way out.
  5. 10:30 — Holy Rosary Church and the riverside warehouses (~30 min) Less than ten minutes further south, the cream spire of Holy Rosary Church, known locally as Kalawar, comes into view. It belongs to a Catholic community of Portuguese descent that moved here from the Thonburi bank in the early Rattanakosin period; the present Gothic building dates from the late reign of King Rama V. The interior opens only for services, but the forecourt and the river path are open all day. Beside the church, old Chinese riverside warehouses have become a café and garden, and this is the best place on the route to sit down, with boats passing in front and the breeze coming off the water.
  6. 11:15 — River City and Si Phraya Pier: the end of the half-day, and two ways home (~5 min) A few hundred metres past the church is River City, the riverside art and antiques centre, with toilets, air-conditioning and coffee for a rest before heading back. The cheap way: walk back along Song Wat to Ratchawong Pier, about 15 minutes, and take the ฿5 ferry to the end of our lane. The ICONSIAM way: take the ICONSIAM shuttle boat from Si Phraya Pier next to River City (there is a fare; it runs 09:00–23:00), have lunch at ICONSIAM, then ride the BTS Gold Line one stop to Khlong San, or take a Grab of about 5 minutes to our door.

Good to know, and our tips

  • Go early, finish before noon: the lanes are narrow and the buildings two or three storeys, so there is shade almost all the way until about 11:00. After midday the sun fills the lanes and the parts shops get busy loading and unloading. For café and street-art photos without people, be at the shrine before 09:00.
  • People work here: the parts shops are not a film set. Motorbikes and handcarts use the lanes all day, so keep to the side and do not stop for photos in the middle. Shopfronts are fine to photograph; ask before you photograph a person. The shrine is open to everyone: hats off, and no photos of people praying. The church interior is for services only, with shoulders and knees covered.
  • Budget for the whole route: ฿10 for the ferry there and back, nothing at the shrine or the church, an entry-plus-drink charge at So Heng Tai at ordinary café prices, one coffee by the river and lunch. Under ฿400 a person is realistic; add the shuttle fare if you return via ICONSIAM. Carry small notes, as many shops in the lanes do not take QR payment.
  • The cafés change often: Talat Noi gains and loses cafés every year, which is why we print no shop names on this page. Ask us before you leave which ones are open at the moment, or follow the signs in the lanes. Most open later than the shrine, so if you arrive at 09:00, walk the lanes first and look for a seat from 10:00 onwards.
  • It joins up with other guides: with a whole day, do Talat Noi in the morning and continue to Song Wat Road in the afternoon when its shops open. If you are already up at dawn, do Sampeng at dawn first and walk through Song Wat into Talat Noi. All three are within walking distance of each other, and the same ferry takes you home.

Common questions

How is this different from the Song Wat Road and Chinatown heritage walk guides?

Song Wat is a single street, best from afternoon into evening for its cafés and galleries. The heritage walk is a long half-day along Chinatown's main roads and famous temples. Talat Noi is the lane district at the far end of Song Wat, about parts shops, old shrines and Chinese houses in the morning. If you can only do one, Talat Noi suits people who like quiet lanes and photography; Song Wat suits people who want to sit in cafés.

Does it work on a Saturday or Sunday?

Yes, and it does not depend on the express boats at all: the route only uses the Tha Din Daeng–Ratchawong ferry, which runs every day, and your feet. The difference is that on weekdays the parts shops are open and the lanes feel like a working district, while at weekends some of them close but there are more cafés open and more photographers about. So Heng Tai and the church keep their own hours; ask us before you set off.

What is the best way back from River City?

If you would rather not walk back, take the ICONSIAM shuttle boat from Si Phraya Pier beside River City (there is a fare) across to ICONSIAM, then ride the BTS Gold Line one stop from Charoen Nakhon to Khlong San and walk to us, or take a Grab of about 5 minutes from outside ICONSIAM. The cheapest option is to walk 15 minutes back to Ratchawong Pier and take the same ฿5 ferry, which lands at the end of our lane.
