Bang Krachao in Half a Day — Across the River to the Green Bend, by Bike on Raised Paths

🚕 Grab ~30–40 min + a short ferry 🚲 ~10–15 km of riding, flat all the way 🛶 Bang Nam Phueng market Sat–Sun only 🕗 Out 08:00 · back ~13:30
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons

Bang Krachao is the green land inside the pig’s-stomach bend of the Chao Phraya, across the river from Khlong Toei. From any tall building in town it looks like a forest in the middle of the city; inside are coconut groves, irrigation channels, wooden houses and the narrow raised concrete paths locals walk and cycle on over the plantation ditches. No high-rises, very few cars. Where our Thonburi-by-bike route stays in the lanes near the hotel, this is the version that goes a little further for real greenery: a half-day morning, one car ride and one short ferry each way.

Why go early, and why on a dry day

Bang Krachao has none of the wide shade of the city; most of the raised paths run through open plantation in full sun. Before 10:00 it is still cool and there are birds to watch in the park; after noon it is too hot to enjoy riding. As for rain: the concrete paths are about a metre wide, often without railings and with moss in places, and slippery when wet — if rain is forecast for the morning, move the day. Bang Nam Phueng floating market only opens on Saturdays and Sundays, so pick a weekend for both the park and the market. On a weekday it is still worth it; you get quiet instead of the market.

The route — from the hotel to the park and the market, and home by early afternoon

  1. 08:00 — Grab from the hotel to Wat Khlong Toei Nok pier (~30–40 min) Pin “Wat Khlong Toei Nok Pier”. The route crosses the river to the Khlong Toei side; weekend mornings are clear, on weekdays allow extra time. Some drivers do not know the pier, so show them the map in the app.
  2. 08:40 — Ferry across to Bang Krachao (~5 min), then rent bikes Take the cross-river ferry from Wat Khlong Toei Nok pier; the fare is a few tens of baht, as posted, and bikes go on board. On the Bang Krachao side there are bike-rental shops around the landing. Pick a bike whose front and rear brakes both work and whose saddle adjusts, and ask for the cycling map — most shops hand one out. A half-day rental is enough.
  3. 09:00 — Raised paths through the plantations to Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park (~30 min) Follow the cycle signs to Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park and Botanical Garden. Most of this stretch is raised concrete path through coconut groves and channels, wide enough to pass if both of you slow down. Ride slowly, ring before bends, and pull in and stop when someone is walking towards you. Where there is no railing and you feel unsure, get off and push — nobody minds.
  4. 09:30 — Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park: a lake, forest boardwalks and a bird tower (~45 min) A large park around a central lake, with wooden walkways under the trees and a tall tower to climb for a view over the canopy to the city towers across the river. Morning is when you see the most birds. Leave the bikes in the marked parking and walk inside; there are toilets and benches. Refill your water here before riding on.
  5. 10:30 — Bang Nam Phueng floating market (Sat–Sun) or a loop through the groves (weekdays) (~1 hour) At the weekend, ride on to Bang Nam Phueng floating market, a community market along a canal selling food, sweets and produce from the local plantations — a good place for a late breakfast. It gets busy mid-morning; park the bikes outside and walk in. On weekdays the market is closed, so use the time for another loop of raised paths from the map, or a rest beside a channel.
  6. 12:00 — Ride back to the pier, return the bikes, ferry across and Grab home (~1 hr 15 min) Follow the signs back to the landing, return the bikes and take the ferry back to Wat Khlong Toei Nok pier. Book a Grab to Khlong San and you are back around 13:30, in time to rest through the hottest part of the afternoon. If you get back after 18:00 it is self check-in — we send the entry steps on LINE or WhatsApp.

Safety, and our tips

  • The raised paths are what to watch: about a metre wide, without railings in places, and shared with local motorbikes. Ride slowly, look well ahead before bends and never ride two abreast. If a wheel slips off the edge, let the bike go and look after yourself first. Children who are not yet confident riders should sit on a child seat or walk the narrow stretches.
  • What to bring: a bottle of water each, sunscreen, a hat and mosquito repellent, especially for the park in the morning. Small notes for the ferry, the bike rental and the market. Some rental shops ask for an ID or a deposit; ask before you take the bike.
  • You can go without cycling: Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park is entirely walkable, and around the landing there are motorbike taxis that will take you to the park or the market. If cycling is not for you, do just the park and come back — shorter and much less tiring.
  • Budget for the whole route: two Grab rides, the ferry there and back as posted, a half-day bike rental at the shop’s rate, and a late breakfast at community-market prices. Shared between several people, the Grab becomes good value. We do not print rental prices because each shop charges differently.
  • Do not stay until dark: many stretches of the raised paths are unlit, and the ferry does not run late. If you want a longer day, plan to be back at the landing at least an hour before sunset, and ask about the last ferry at the pier when you arrive.

Common questions

How is this different from Thonburi by bike?

Thonburi by bike stays in the lanes and riverside temples near the hotel; you arrange a bike in advance but need no car. Bang Krachao needs about half an hour by car and a ferry, but gives you plantations, raised paths and a quiet the city does not have, with rental bikes waiting at the landing. With only one half-day and a wish for greenery, choose Bang Krachao.

Is it worth going on a weekday?

Yes. Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park and the raised paths are open every day, and weekdays are far quieter and easier to ride. What you miss is Bang Nam Phueng floating market, which only opens on Saturdays and Sundays; on a weekday, have your late breakfast at a roadside stall or near the landing instead.

Can we go by boat from near the hotel, without a car?

There is no scheduled boat from a pier near the hotel straight to Bang Krachao that we can recommend for every day. The Chao Phraya express boats do not stop on the Bang Krachao side, and currently run on weekdays only. The simplest, most predictable way is a Grab to Wat Khlong Toei Nok pier and the cross-river ferry. If you are interested in a chartered boat or a tour, message us.
