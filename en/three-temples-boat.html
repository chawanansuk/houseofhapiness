Wat Pho, Wat Arun & Wat Kalayanamit — Three Temples in Half a Day, Mostly by River

🚤 Three temples linked by boat · ferries cost a few baht 🛕 3 temples · ~4–5 hours 🌅 Go early — cooler, temples quieter 👕 Dress modestly — cover shoulders and knees
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

Three riverside temples close enough to link entirely by boat: the gilded Reclining Buddha at Wat Pho, the porcelain prang of Wat Arun across the water, and the giant seated Buddha at Wat Kalayanamit on the Thonburi bank. Our hotel sits on the same bank of the river. Start in the morning and ~4–5 hours covers all three before you loop back to rest.

Why do it by river

These three temples grew up with the river, so the prettiest and easiest way to reach them is by boat, not road — no traffic, you see each temple from the water the way people did a century ago, and the cross-river ferries cost almost nothing. Our hotel sits on the Thonburi bank of the Chao Phraya, the same side as Wat Arun and Wat Kalayanamit, with the pier at the end of our lane. So we string the trip together with short ferry and express-boat hops: Wat Pho on the Phra Nakhon side, across to Wat Arun, then south to Wat Kalayanamit before looping home. If you'd rather not change boats several times, a Grab fills any leg.

The route

  1. 8:00 AM — Leave the hotel and head for Tha Tien pier (Wat Pho). Walk to the pier at the end of the lane, take the ฿5 ferry to the Phra Nakhon side and connect to a northbound Chao Phraya express boat to Tha Tien — or simply take a Grab to the temple gate. Going this early keeps the sun soft and the temple uncrowded.
  2. 8:30 AM — Wat Pho: the Reclining Buddha and Thai massage. The highlight is the gilded Reclining Buddha, around 46 metres long, its soles inlaid with 108 auspicious symbols. Wander the cluster of stupas and the old medical inscriptions. Wat Pho is the home of the Thai-massage tradition, so if you have time a 30-minute foot massage before moving on is wonderfully reviving. (There is an entry fee for foreign visitors here.)
  3. 10:00 AM — Cross-river ferry: Tha Tien → Wat Arun. From Wat Pho walk to Tha Tien pier and take the little cross-river ferry to Wat Arun opposite — a few baht and a few minutes. The prang grows taller as the boat noses in, one of the most classic photo angles in Bangkok.
  4. 10:20 AM — Wat Arun: climb the riverside prang. The spire is encrusted with countless pieces of glazed porcelain and seashell. You can climb the steep steps to the mid-level (hold the rail) and look back across the river to Wat Pho. It is beautiful by day and at dusk alike; there is a foreign-visitor entry fee here too.
  5. 11:30 AM — South to Wat Kalayanamit (Thonburi bank). Wat Kalayanamit sits on the river just south, on our Thonburi side. Reach it by a short cross-river ferry hop, a walk, or a quick Grab. It is quieter and far less crowded than the first two, with a calm riverside feel.
  6. 11:50 AM — Wat Kalayanamit: the giant seated Buddha. The main hall enshrines a large Buddha image locals call "Luang Pho To" (Sam Po Kong), especially revered by Thai-Chinese devotees, and the temple has a distinctly Chinese feel. Nearby lie the Kudi Chin quarter and Santa Cruz Church — if your legs hold out, walk on for the old Kudi Chin Portuguese-style cakes.
  7. 12:45 PM — Loop back to the hotel. Several ways home from Wat Kalayanamit: a northbound express boat then the cross-river ferry at Ratchawong, or a Grab back over the bridge to Khlong San in ~10–15 minutes. You're home in the early afternoon — hide from the heat, then head out again in the evening.

Small tips from us

  • Dress to pass the temple gate: every temple asks for covered shoulders and knees — no tank tops or above-knee shorts. Keep a light scarf in your bag just in case.
  • Mornings win: before 9 AM the sun is gentle and crowds thin. Wat Pho and Wat Arun get packed and very hot by mid-morning.
  • Carry cash: ferry fares and temple entry are cash — keep small notes and coins ready.
  • Slip-on shoes: you remove shoes to enter the ordination halls, so easy-off footwear saves a lot of fuss.
  • Shorten it freely: short on time or too hot, do just Wat Pho and Wat Arun — they face each other — and save Wat Kalayanamit for another day.

Common questions

Is there an entry fee for the temples, and how much?

Wat Pho and Wat Arun charge a foreign-visitor entry fee, while Wat Kalayanamit is normally free to enter and pray. Fees are revised from time to time, so check on site or ask us before you go to be sure.

Can it really be done by boat alone?

Almost the whole route works on cross-river ferries and the Chao Phraya express boats, but some legs mean several changes and waiting for the next boat. To save time or if you're a group, a Grab on one or two legs is more convenient.
