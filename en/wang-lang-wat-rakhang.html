Half a day by boat · northern Thonburi

Wang Lang, Siriraj and Wat Rakhang — a Riverside Market, Two Museums and a Temple of Bells, Two Boats from Us

⛴️ ฿5 ferry + blue-flag boat to Phran Nok 📅 Works every day, no express boat needed 🕘 09:00–13:30 · ~2 km on foot 🏥 Siriraj museums charge entry · Wat Rakhang is free
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons

Northern Thonburi, between Khlong Bangkok Noi and Wat Rakhang, is a neighbourhood every Bangkokian knows and almost no foreign visitor reaches. Wang Lang market is a riverside lane where Siriraj doctors, nurses and students eat every day; the Siriraj hospital museums tell the story of the district and of Thai medicine; and Wat Rakhang Khositaram is the temple where people ring bells for luck all day long. Everything is within walking distance of everything else, and from us it takes one cross-river ferry and one blue-flag boat. Because the blue flag calls at Phran Nok every day, this route works even at weekends, when the express boats do not run.

Why this route is better than it sounds

Because it packs boats, food, history and a temple into half a day with almost no road travel, and with a fraction of the crowds on the Grand Palace bank directly opposite. The blue-flag boat runs upriver past Wat Arun, Tha Tien and Tha Chang to Phran Nok, which sits right at the head of Wang Lang market. From the market you walk through the hospital grounds to the museums, then about 15 minutes south along the river to Wat Rakhang. For the way back, a cross-river ferry runs from Wat Rakhang pier to Tha Chang, where the blue flag heads back downriver to Ratchawong — no retracing your steps. Tha Chang is also the pier we use for the Grand Palace, if you want to stretch this into a full day.

The route — Tha Din Daeng to Wat Rakhang, and home via Tha Chang

  1. 09:00 — Tha Din Daeng → Ratchawong (฿5 ferry · ~3 min) Walk from the hotel to the pier at the end of our lane, pay ฿5 and cross to Ratchawong. Stay in the pier: the blue-flag tourist boat ticket window is right there.
  2. 09:15 — Ratchawong → Phran Nok (blue-flag boat · ~25–30 min) Buy a single or a day ticket at the window (ask the price there). The boat runs upriver past Wat Arun, Tha Tien, Tha Chang and Tha Maharaj, then crosses to Phran Nok on the Thonburi bank. Sit on the left for the closest view of Wat Arun; towards the end you see the Grand Palace walls across the water. Monday to Friday the orange-flag express boat is another option — ask the pier staff which boat stops at Wang Lang.
  3. 09:45 — Wang Lang market: Siriraj’s riverside lane (~40 min) Step off at Phran Nok and you are straight into the lanes of Wang Lang market: rice shops, sweets, fruit and inexpensive clothes on both sides, running all the way to Wang Lang pier in front of the hospital. The people squeezing past are Siriraj staff and students, so this is a working market, not a tourist one. Before 11:00 it is easy to walk; after noon it is packed for the lunch break. Have a late breakfast here.
  4. 10:30 — The Siriraj museums: the district’s history and the history of medicine (~1 hour) Follow the museum signs into the Siriraj hospital grounds. There are two to choose from. The Siriraj Bimuksthan Museum, in the old railway station building on Khlong Bangkok Noi, tells the story of this area from the Thonburi period to the hospital — good for everyone. The Siriraj Medical Museum covers forensic medicine and pathology and displays real human specimens — not for small children or the squeamish. Both charge admission and close on certain weekdays, so check the Siriraj website before you go. This is a working hospital: walk quietly and never photograph patients.
  5. 11:45 — Walk south to Wat Rakhang Khositaram (~15 min), then the temple (~40 min) Leave the hospital and walk south on the roads and lanes along the river for about a kilometre to Wat Rakhang. True to its name — rakhang means bell — the courtyard has rows of bells you can ring for luck: tap gently, a few strokes at a time. Thai visitors come to pay respects at the statue of Somdet Phra Phutthachan (To Phromrangsi), a former abbot revered across the country. Inside the grounds are a wooden Tripitaka library linked to the life of King Rama I and a prang beside the courtyard. Free entry; dress modestly and take your shoes off before entering the ordination hall. At the pier in front, people like to feed the fish.
  6. 12:30 — Wat Rakhang pier → Tha Chang → Ratchawong → home (~45 min) Take the cross-river ferry from the pier in front of the temple to Tha Chang, a few minutes (fare on the board). At Tha Chang, board the blue-flag boat downriver to Ratchawong, about 20 minutes, then the ฿5 ferry to the end of our lane. If you still have energy and the palace is open, Tha Chang is the start of our Grand Palace by boat route, but the afternoon sun is fierce and we suggest keeping it for another morning. If you are tired, a Grab from outside Wat Rakhang back to Khlong San takes about 15–25 minutes depending on traffic.

Good to know, and our tips

  • Go in the morning, avoid the hospital lunch hour: Wang Lang market is at its most crowded from about 11:30 to 13:00, when Siriraj staff all come out to eat. Walk the market before 11:00 and be in the museums during the rush.
  • Etiquette in the hospital and the temple: Siriraj is a working hospital with real patients. Keep your voice down, do not photograph patients or relatives, and follow the signed routes. At Wat Rakhang, cover shoulders and knees, ring the bells gently rather than hammering them, and do not photograph people praying.
  • Days to watch: both museums close on certain weekdays and some public holidays, so check before you go if they are the point of the trip. On a closed day the route still works: drop the museum stop and spend longer in the market and at Wat Rakhang.
  • Budget for the whole route: two ฿5 ferry rides, two blue-flag fares or a day pass (ask at the window), the Wat Rakhang–Tha Chang ferry as posted, museum entry as posted, and a late breakfast at Wang Lang at ordinary local prices. Wat Rakhang is free. Carry small notes for the boats and the market stalls.
  • It joins up with other guides: for another half-day, use the next morning for the Grand Palace by boat from the same pier, or come back in the evening and walk Kudi Chin and the Sky Park, which are on the Thonburi side closer to the hotel.

Common questions

Does it work at the weekend, when the express boats do not run?

Yes. The route uses the cross-river ferries and the blue-flag tourist boat, separate services from the express boats that run every day. The blue flag calls at Phran Nok according to the operator’s published list, and the Wat Rakhang–Tha Chang ferry also runs daily. At weekends Wang Lang market is quieter because the hospital is, but some stalls in the lanes are closed.

Is the Siriraj Medical Museum disturbing? Can we bring children?

The forensic and pathology sections display real human specimens and material from real cases. Most adults are fine; some people find it upsetting, and we do not recommend it for young children. With a family, choose the Siriraj Bimuksthan Museum, which covers the history of the area and is suitable for all ages, and spend longer at the market and Wat Rakhang.

Can we do the route in reverse?

Yes. Take the blue flag to Tha Chang, cross to Wat Rakhang in the morning when it is quietest, then walk north to Siriraj and Wang Lang market and come back on the blue flag from Phran Nok. The catch is that you reach Wang Lang market right in the hospital lunch hour, when it is at its most crowded.
