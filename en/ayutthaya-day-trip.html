Ayutthaya in One Day from Bangkok — Out Early from Khlong San, Back by the River at Night

🚆 An easy day return · ~80 km north · roughly 1–2 hours each way 🛕 Four to six main temples is a full day — you don't need to see them all 🚲 On the old island: rent a bicycle, hire a tuk-tuk, or keep the car you came in ☀️ Leave the hotel around 6:30 AM, back around 7–8 PM
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

Ayutthaya was the capital for 417 years before the capital moved downriver to Thonburi and then Bangkok, and it sits about 80 km north of our door, an easy day return. Red-brick stupas, the sandstone Buddha head wrapped in banyan roots, and a riverside temple that looks its best in late-afternoon light — all of it fits into one day if you leave early and pick your temples sensibly. This is the rhythm we give our guests, from stepping out of Tha Din Daeng lane in the morning to a shower back on the Chao Phraya the same night.

Why Ayutthaya is worth giving up a whole day for

Bangkok is full of beautiful temples, but Ayutthaya gives you something Bangkok cannot: a ruined city. Half-standing brick walls, stupas whose spires came down when the capital fell, rows of headless Buddha images across open grass. It is quiet and wide-open, and it explains where everything you have been looking at in Bangkok came from. Staying with us on the Thonburi bank helps: we are on the same river that flows past Ayutthaya, and it is only a few minutes from our lane to the train. Out early and back at night means no packing, no checking out, no overnight bag — you come home to the same room, shower, and walk out for dinner by the water like any other evening.

The one-day timeline — from our front door to Ayutthaya and back

  1. 6:30 AM — Leave the hotel. One extra early hour always pays for itself on this trip. From Tha Din Daeng 16 walk to the Gold Line at Khlong San, ride two stops to Krung Thonburi, then continue by train across town to whichever departure station you have chosen. If there are three or more of you, or you have children and camera bags, a single Grab from the mouth of the lane is simpler and not much more expensive. Leave plenty of slack — Bangkok traffic thickens earlier than visitors expect.
  2. 7:30 AM — Pick your way north: train, minivan or private car. The train on the northern line is the classic: several departures a day, cheap, windows open over rice fields — but timings slip, and most long-distance services now leave from Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal at Bang Sue, with some still using the old terminus. Always check the departure station and schedule before you go. A minivan or bus from the bus terminals is faster and leaves often, though seats are tight and many services depart only once full. A private car with a driver is the most comfortable, reaches more temples in less time, and makes real sense for three or four people. Ask us at check-in and we will help you check the current options.
  3. 9:00 AM — Arrive and sort out wheels for the old city. The ruins are spread across an island ringed by rivers, and the gaps between them are too long to walk all day in that heat. Most people rent a bicycle or a scooter near the station or in town, hire a tuk-tuk by the hour or half-day, or simply keep the driver they came with. Whichever you choose, agree the price and the list of temples before you set off.
  4. 9:30 AM — Wat Mahathat and Wat Ratchaburana. Start in the middle of the island while the light is still soft. Wat Mahathat holds the sandstone Buddha head cradled in tree roots that has become the image of the whole province — to photograph it you must crouch so your own head stays lower than the Buddha's, a rule site staff enforce and simple good manners besides. Then cross the road to Wat Ratchaburana, where the prang still stands tall with a steep, narrow stair up inside it (the stair isn't always open).
  5. 11:30 AM — Wat Phra Si Sanphet, Wihan Phra Mongkhon Bophit, then lunch. The three bell-shaped stupas in a row inside the old palace grounds are Ayutthaya's postcard. Next door stands Wihan Phra Mongkhon Bophit with its huge bronze Buddha, still prayed to daily — not a ruin but a living temple. Around here there is shade, shops and restaurants for the hottest hour. The local things to eat are boat noodles, river prawns, and the cotton-candy roti the town is known for, which travels home well as a gift.
  6. 2:30 PM — Wat Chaiwatthanaram and Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon. Wat Chaiwatthanaram sits off the island on the riverbank, a central prang ringed by smaller towers in a layout that borrows from Khmer temple plans — and it is at its best from mid-afternoon into evening, when low sun catches the brick. If you still have the energy, cross town to Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon, whose enormous stupa is visible from far off, with steps to climb and a white Reclining Buddha in saffron cloth in the same grounds. The two are on opposite sides of the city; picking only one is a perfectly good plan.
  7. 5:00 PM — Head back to Bangkok. Allow more slack on the return than on the way out, especially at weekends and on long holidays when everyone travels home at once. If you came by train or minivan, note the last departure on your way out. You will reach Bangkok in the evening and retrace the route to Khlong San — train across town, then two Gold Line stops, or a Grab straight to the mouth of our lane. Home by about 8 PM. Our staff are here until 6 PM; after that you let yourself in as usual, and if you are not too tired there is still supper to be found by the water.

Small tips from us

  • The sun is the real opponent: the ruins are open brick with almost no shade. A wide hat, sunscreen, sunglasses and a big bottle of water each are necessities, not extras. Between noon and 2 PM, stay in the shade or in a restaurant rather than pushing on.
  • The ruins are still sacred ground: cover shoulders and knees as you would at a Bangkok temple, never climb on stupas or brick walls, do not sit on the monuments, and at Wat Mahathat crouch below the Buddha head for photos. Wardens do enforce this, and fines are possible.
  • Carry cash and ask about the combined ticket: most sites charge entry individually and mostly in cash, and a combined multi-site ticket is sold at the ticket windows at times — worth it if you plan on several temples. Prices and conditions change, so ask at the first ticket window you reach.
  • Match the transport to your group: two people who want the atmosphere — take the train. Want speed and frequent departures — minivan. Travelling with children, older parents, or as a group of three or four — a private car wins outright and lets you rewrite the plan mid-day. All three work as a day return.
  • Don't try to see everything: there are hundreds of sites here, and people who chase them all remember nothing but the heat. Choose four to six, walk them slowly, sit under a tree for a while, and leave the rest for next time.

Common questions

Is a single day really enough?

Comfortably, if you leave us around 6:30 AM and aim for four to six temples. Travel runs roughly 1–2 hours each way depending how you go, which leaves most of the day in the old city. The pleasure of doing it in a day is that you never check out or move a bag — you come back to the same room by the river that night.

Train, minivan, or hire a car?

The train has the best atmosphere and the smallest fare, but timings are loose and you still need wheels once you arrive. Minivans are quick and frequent but cramped. A private car with a driver is the most comfortable and flexible, and suits families or groups. Schedules, departure stations and prices all change from time to time — tell us at check-in which style you prefer and we'll help you check the details.

Is there an entry fee, and how should we dress?

The main sites charge entry individually, and a combined multi-site ticket is sometimes available; prices are revised from time to time, so bring cash and ask at the first ticket window. For clothing, cover shoulders and knees as at any temple, and wear shoes you can walk on brick and gravel in — ideally ones that slip off easily, since some halls ask you to remove them.
