One Full Day in Thonburi — starting from Khlong San

🕗 Morning to night 🚶 Walk + boat + BTS 💸 ~฿150-350 transport
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

Thonburi is the Bangkok most visitors miss — riverside temples, centuries-old communities and real local markets, all a few minutes on foot, by boat or by train from our hotel. This is the one-day route we recommend to every guest.

The full-day route

  1. 08:00 — Breakfast like a local: old-style coffee and patongko around Tha Din Daeng (spots in our neighbourhood guide), then wander a genuine morning market with zero tourists.
  2. 09:30 — Wat Arun: ~10 minutes to the pier, then the cross-river ferry. Go early for soft light and thin crowds. Dress modestly (rentals at the gate).
  3. 11:30 — Kudi Chin: the 250-year-old Portuguese quarter — Santa Cruz Church and charcoal-baked khanom farang cakes from the original family bakeries.
  4. 13:00 — Lunch + Sky Park: noodles near Memorial Bridge, then the Chao Phraya Sky Park — the world's first park built on a river bridge, with 360° views.
  5. 15:30 — ICONSIAM: one Gold Line stop — air-con, the indoor floating-market food hall (Sook Siam), and a rest before the finale.
  6. 18:30 — Finish in Chinatown: ~10 minutes across the bridge for legendary street food late into the night, then a 10-minute Grab back to bed.

Tips for a smooth day

  • Best days: Tuesday-Sunday (many Yaowarat stalls close Mondays). In the rainy season, rearrange the plan so the mall and indoor community stops fall in the afternoon, when showers are most likely.
  • Dress: shoulders and knees covered for temples; comfortable shoes matter most — you'll walk 6-8 km.
  • Nap anytime: the perk of staying in the neighbourhood — nothing on this route is more than 15 minutes from your room.

FAQ

Does this route work on a Monday?

It works, but it is not the best day: a fair number of Yaowarat places close on Mondays. Tuesday to Sunday is easier. If Monday is your only day, swap the evening leg for the Thonburi riverside instead.

How much walking is this?

Roughly 6–8 km across the whole day, broken into short stretches between stops rather than one long march. Comfortable shoes matter more than anything else here, and the temples require covered shoulders and knees.

Can I come back to rest in the middle of the day?

From any stop. Nothing on this route is more than 15 minutes from the hotel, so an afternoon nap before the evening leg is easy. It is what we suggest to guests travelling with small children or older parents.
