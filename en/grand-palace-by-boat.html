The Grand Palace in Half a Day — a ฿5 Ferry, a Boat to Tha Chang, and In Before the Crowds

⛴️ Two boats: the ฿5 ferry, then the blue flag to Tha Chang 🕗 At the gate for opening ~8:30 AM · out before 11 👖 Shoulders and knees covered · long trousers 🚫 Anyone outside saying “the palace is closed today” — keep walking
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons

The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew are where nearly every guest of ours is heading, and the place they complain most about traffic and heat. From Khlong San there is a way to dodge both: the ฿5 ferry from the pier at the end of our lane to Ratchawong, the blue-flag tourist boat upriver to Tha Chang, and about five minutes on foot to the gate. Under an hour door to door, and not a minute of it on a road. This page gets you there as the gates open, explains the dress code that is never waived, and tells you what to do about the people who insist the palace is closed.

Why by boat, and why early

The palace sits on the Phra Nakhon bank, diagonally upriver from our side of Thonburi. The roads around it are among the most congested on Rattanakosin island by mid-morning, while the river runs straight from Ratchawong to Tha Chang in about 20 minutes whatever the traffic is doing. As for timing: the palace opens in the morning and stops selling tickets in the afternoon, the tour groups arrive around 9:30–10:00, and by midday the sun on the marble courtyards is brutal. Leave us before 7:45 and you walk in as the gates open, see Wat Phra Kaew while there is still room to stand, and come out for lunch as everyone else is joining the ticket queue.

The route — Tha Din Daeng to the Emerald Buddha and home before noon

  1. 7:45 AM — Tha Din Daeng → Ratchawong (฿5 ferry · ~3 min). About 5 minutes on foot from the hotel to the pier at the end of our lane. Pay ฿5 at the booth and board; the crossing to Ratchawong on the Chinatown side takes under 3 minutes. Don't leave the pier — your second boat departs from here too. Photograph the ferry timetable board for the way back.
  2. 8:00 AM — Ratchawong → Tha Chang (blue-flag tourist boat · ~20 min). The tourist boat has a ticket window at Ratchawong; it calls here roughly every 30 minutes and runs upriver past Pak Khlong Talat, Wat Arun and Tha Tien to Tha Chang, the pier closest to the palace gate. Ask for the first morning departure and the single-ride fare at the window (a day pass is better value only if you plan to hop off at several piers). The boat passes close in front of Wat Arun — sit on the left for the clearest view. Monday to Friday the ฿19 orange-flag express boat is an alternative; ask the pier staff whether the next one stops at Tha Chang. At weekends the blue flag is the only option.
  3. 8:25 AM — Tha Chang → the palace gate (~5 min on foot). Off the pier, through the little market in front of it and out onto Na Phra Lan Road; turn left and the long white wall on your right is the palace. Follow it to the Wiset Chaisri Gate, the visitors' entrance. This stretch is where people will approach you to say the palace is closed today, closed for the morning, or closed for a ceremony, and offer to take you somewhere else. Don't engage; keep walking to the gate. If the palace really is closed, the only announcements are the official notice at the gate and the palace website.
  4. 8:30 AM — Tickets, then straight to Wat Phra Kaew (~1 hour). The ticket windows are just past the dress-code check. One ticket covers Wat Phra Kaew, the outer court of the palace and the Queen Sirikit Museum of Textiles; the foreign-visitor price is on the board at the window (it changes, so we don't print it — Thai citizens enter free with an ID card). Once inside, go directly to Wat Phra Kaew before the tour groups arrive. The ubosot holds the Emerald Buddha: shoes off before you go up, no photographs inside, sit with your feet tucked away from the image. The cloister around the temple carries the Ramakien murals through all 178 panels — walk the shaded side first, before the sun climbs.
  5. 9:40 AM — The outer court: Chakri Maha Prasat and the throne halls (~45 min). Leave Wat Phra Kaew by its southern side into the palace courtyard. Chakri Maha Prasat — a European building wearing Thai spired roofs — is the palace in every photograph you have seen. Beyond it stands Dusit Maha Prasat; both are seen from outside and, where open, partly within (the inner court is never open to visitors). Before you leave, if you have the energy, the Museum of Textiles near the entrance is air-conditioned — a good place to cool down before going back out into the sun.
  6. 10:30 AM — Out before the crowds; choose your way home. The shortest: walk back to Tha Chang, take the blue-flag boat downriver to Ratchawong (~20 min) and the ferry across — home before noon. The better-value option if you still have legs: walk south along Maha Rat Road ~10 minutes to Wat Pho and the reclining Buddha, then board at Tha Tien for Ratchawong. We cover that in detail in our three riverside temples route. If the heat has beaten you, a Grab from outside Tha Chang back to Khlong San over Phra Pok Klao Bridge takes ~15–25 minutes depending on traffic.

Rules that are never waived, and our tips

  • The dress code is enforced at the gate. Trousers or a skirt covering the knees, sleeves covering the shoulders; no vests, no leggings or sheer fabrics, no ripped jeans. Sarongs can be borrowed or bought near the entrance, but the queue is long by mid-morning — easier to dress right from the hotel. Sandals are fine; you take them off before entering the ubosot anyway.
  • “The palace is closed today” is a sentence to walk past. The people saying it around Tha Chang and Na Phra Lan Road will offer a cheap tuk-tuk to another temple or a gem shop instead. The palace opens every day except for royal ceremonies, which are announced in advance at the gate and on the Bureau of the Royal Household website. If in doubt, message us before you leave.
  • Hours and special closures. The palace opens in the morning and ticket sales end in the afternoon, around half past three. We don't print fixed times because they shift for royal ceremonies — check the palace website the night before, or let us check at check-in.
  • Budget and cash. Two ฿5 ferry rides, two single tourist-boat fares or a day pass (ask at the window), the palace ticket as posted, and water. Carry small notes for the boats; the palace ticket windows take cards, but cash is quicker when the queue is long.
  • Sun and water. The palace courtyards are marble and tile with almost no shade; a hat, sunglasses and a bottle of water each are genuinely necessary. Water is sold inside at a premium — buy it at a convenience store near the hotel before you board.

Common questions

Can we still go by boat at the weekend, when the express boats don't run?

Yes. This route uses the cross-river ferry and the blue-flag tourist boat, which are separate services from the express boats and run every day. The ferry leaves constantly through the morning and the blue flag calls at Ratchawong roughly every 30 minutes. Weekend boats are fuller than weekday ones, so be at Ratchawong before 8:00 AM for one of the early runs with seats to spare.

How long does it all take, and is it manageable with children or older travellers?

Leave at 7:45 and you're back around noon without Wat Pho — four hours in all. Inside the palace you walk about 2 km on flat ground with no shade. Older travellers manage well if they go early and rest in the air-conditioned textile museum. Small children usually flag after an hour, so aim for Wat Phra Kaew and Chakri Maha Prasat and then head home. Hold children's hands on and off the boats, and wait until the boat is still before stepping.

Can we combine it with Wat Arun and Wat Pho the same day?

You can, but we don't recommend all three before noon. The palace alone takes two hours and is more tiring than it looks because of the sun. For the full set, do the palace at opening, Wat Pho at 11:00, then come home to rest, and keep Wat Arun for the evening of another day — you see it close up from the boat anyway.
