Electric boats in Bangkok — which ones work from Khlong San

⚡ River e-boats calling on the Chinatown bank 💳 Tap-to-pay cards on many routes 🛶 Canal e-boat taxis booked in an app ⛴️ Start with the ฿5 ferry
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

Bangkok has two kinds of electric boat a visitor can use: electric scheduled boats on the Chao Phraya, and small electric boat taxis on the old canals in the city centre, booked in an app much like a car. Both can start from the Chinatown bank — just a ฿5 ferry from the pier at the end of our lane.

On the river: electric scheduled boats

The electric boats on the Chao Phraya run as scheduled lines, each with its own colour. They're air-conditioned, quieter than the diesel boats, and many routes take tap-to-pay debit or credit cards. The information we've gathered says some lines call at Ratchawong on the Chinatown bank — one ferry ride from Tha Din Daeng. Check the board on the pier for electric boats and the next departure.

  • Where to boardTake the ฿5 ferry to Ratchawong and look for the electric-boat board, or go to Sathorn Pier (Saphan Taksin), where several lines begin.
  • How to payMany lines take tap-to-pay cards, and you can also pay the conductor on board — keep some cash in case a card doesn't work.
  • FaresTens of baht per ride; the current fare is on the pier board.

On the canals: electric boat taxis

Since early 2026, small electric boat taxis have run on Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem and Khlong Bang Lamphu, calling at piers by places like Hua Lamphong, Nang Loeng Market, Phra Sumen Fort and Ratchadamnoen. They run in the daytime only and are booked and paid through the MuvMi app — a good way to see the old canals and waterside neighbourhoods that cars can't reach.

  • Getting there from the hotelTake the ferry to Ratchawong, walk to MRT Wat Mangkon in Chinatown, ride the Blue Line one stop to Hua Lamphong, then open the app to find the nearest pier.
  • You need the MuvMi appSign up before you leave the hotel. Several users report that registration needs a Thai mobile number for the code — if it won't go through, use the river boats instead.
  • FaresTens of baht per person; the app shows the price before you confirm.

Small tips from us

  • Photograph the pier board: electric-boat lines and times still change often — keep a photo of the timetable.
  • Carry backup cash: many lines take cards, but a reader can be down on any given day.
  • Canal boats are for daytime: the canal taxis don't run in the evening — plan them for late morning to afternoon.
  • The last ferry home: be back at Ratchawong before the last ferry at 20:30, or take a Grab over the bridge.

Common questions

Can I pay with a foreign card?

Many river e-boat lines take tap-to-pay debit or credit cards — keep some cash as a backup. The canal boat taxis are paid in the MuvMi app.

How are the electric boats different from the express boats?

The electric boats are separate lines from the Chao Phraya express boats and stop at a different set of piers. They're air-conditioned, quieter and take cards, while the express boats are split by flag colour and mostly take cash.

Do they run when it rains?

Usually yes, but heavy rain or rough water can change the schedule. Check the pier board or message us to help re-plan.
