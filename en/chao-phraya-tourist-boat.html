Hop-on hop-off · The blue-flag boat

The Chao Phraya hop-on hop-off boat — is it worth it, and where to board near us

🎫 A day ticket, on and off at many piers 🚩 The blue-flag boat, separate from the commuter lines 🎙️ Commentary along the way ⛴️ A ฿5 ferry puts you on the Chinatown bank
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

If you want to stop at several places along the river in one day — Wat Arun, Tha Maharaj, Pak Khlong Talat, ending at ICONSIAM — the blue-flag tourist boat is built for exactly that. One ticket lets you hop on and off at its piers all day, with commentary on board. It costs more than the commuter boats, but you don't have to work out which flag colour to catch.

How it differs from the express boats

TopicBlue-flag tourist boatChao Phraya express boat
TicketA day ticket, on and off many times — a few hundred bahtPay per ride — tens of baht
PiersOnly the main sightseeing piersMany piers, depending on the flag
CommentaryYesNo
Best forThree or more stops in a dayOne place and back

When it's worth it

It pays off when you'll get off three or more times in a day and want commentary on the way. If you're only visiting Wat Pho and Wat Arun, the cross-river ferry and the express boat are far cheaper — see our three-temples-by-boat guide.

A simple ruleCount your stops. Fewer than three: take the commuter boats. Three or more and you'd rather not change boats yourself: take the tourist boat.

Boarding from our side

  • Cross to Ratchawong firstThe ferry from Tha Din Daeng at the end of our lane to Ratchawong is ฿5 and about 3 minutes. The Chinatown-side pier is a main pier where several boat services stop — check the board to see whether the blue-flag boat calls there and when the next one leaves.
  • Or start at ICONSIAMICONSIAM is on our side of the river, one Gold Line stop away, and the pier in front of the mall is another main sightseeing pier.
  • Buy at the pier windowAsk at the tourist-boat ticket window on the pier for the day-ticket and evening-ticket prices and the last boat — they change from time to time.

A half-day plan from the hotel

  1. 9:30 AM — Ferry to Ratchawong: ฿5, about 3 minutes; buy a day ticket at the tourist-boat window.
  2. 10:00 AM — Wat Arun: see the prang before the sun gets fierce.
  3. 11:30 AM — Tha Maharaj: lunch by the water and a break from the heat.
  4. 1:00 PM — Pak Khlong Talat: the flower market, colourful at any hour.
  5. 2:30 PM — ICONSIAM or home: ride on down to ICONSIAM and take the Gold Line back, or get off at Ratchawong and cross home by ferry.

Small tips from us

  • Start early: the riverside temples get hot by midday, so make the most of the day ticket from the morning.
  • Photograph the timetable: snap the board at the pier so you know the next boat at every stop.
  • Temple dress: cover shoulders and knees; keep a light scarf with you.
  • Rain or rough water: sailings can change — ask at the pier before you buy.

Common questions

Where do I buy a ticket?

At the tourist-boat ticket window on any pier where it stops. Ask there for the day-ticket and evening-ticket prices and the time of the last boat.

Is it better value than the express boat?

It's worth it when you'll get off three or more times in a day and want the commentary. For one or two stops, the ferry and the express boat are far cheaper.

Does it run when it rains?

Usually yes, but in heavy rain or rough water the sailings can change. Ask at the pier before you buy, or message us for an indoor plan B.
