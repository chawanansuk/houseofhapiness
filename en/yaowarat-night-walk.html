Night Walk · Yaowarat, Bangkok Chinatown

Yaowarat Night Food Walk — 2 Hours, a ฿5 Ferry Ride from Our Door

🚤 ฿5 cross-river ferry 🚶 ~3 km on foot · 2 hours 🕒 Leave 6 PM, at the pier by ~8:15 PM 💸 ~฿300-500 per person
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons · credits

Yaowarat Road is 1.5 km long. The neon comes on at 6 PM and the carts stay out past midnight. Our hotel is directly across the river: a 3-minute ferry lands you at the start of the street. This is the 6-stop eating route we lay out for guests to cover in about 2 hours, taste 4 or 5 things without doubling back, and sleep on the quiet side.

How this differs from the usual Yaowarat guide

Most guides hand you 20 restaurant names and leave you to hunt. In one evening you can realistically eat 4 or 5 things, so we cut it down to one loop of 6 stops from the pier and back, never walking the same stretch twice. Each stop is chosen by type of food and position on the street, not tied to a single vendor, because carts move and take different days off. A way to pick a stall that works any night is in stop 3. For the daytime version with shrines, old shophouses and Talat Noi, use our Chinatown heritage walk. This page is the night version, food first.

The route in 6 stops (6 PM to ~8:15 PM)

  1. 6:00 PM — Tha Din Daeng → Ratchawong pier (฿5 ferry, 3 minutes). About 5 minutes' walk from the hotel to the pier at the end of our lane. Buy a ticket at the booth with coins or small notes. Before boarding, read the last-boat time on the pier sign and photograph it. The sky is changing colour now; stand on the left side for Phra Pok Klao Bridge and the riverside lights.
  2. 6:10 PM — Ratchawong Road up to Yaowarat Road (~350 m, 5 minutes). From the pier walk straight up Ratchawong Road, past wholesalers closing and soy-milk stalls opening. At the junction with Yaowarat Road turn right. This corner is where the right-hand line of carts begins. Don't eat here yet; walk 200 metres first to see which stalls have the queues tonight.
  3. 6:30 PM — Mid-Yaowarat: two small bites. The ~500 m between the Ratchawong junction and Soi Phadung Dao is the densest stretch of carts. Eat two things you can hold standing: a small bowl of kuay jab (peppery rolled-noodle soup) and either a crispy oyster omelette off the iron griddle or a khanom bueang crepe, shared between two. How to pick a stall: look for a queue of Thais, an owner cooking rather than hired hands ladling, and a price board. No board, ask before ordering.
  4. 7:05 PM — Soi Phadung Dao: the sit-down main course (30-40 minutes). A short lane off the left of Yaowarat where wok-seafood restaurants fill the road with tables. Two or three big names compete at the mouth of the lane at similar prices: curry-powder crab, prawns baked with glass noodles, steamed sea bass with lime, plain rice. Two dishes for two people is enough, dessert is still coming. If the queue at the front is over 15 minutes, walk 50 metres deeper into the lane: smaller places, same menu.
  5. 7:50 PM — The Chinatown Gate at Odeon Circle (~400 m, 6 minutes). Continue to the eastern end of Yaowarat Road. The red-and-gold Chinese gate on the roundabout is the brightest night photo spot in the district; stand across the road to get the gate and the Yaowarat neon in one frame. From here walk back along the other side of the street (your left, facing the way you came) so you pass none of the same stalls.
  6. 8:05 PM — The Plaeng Nam side and the way back: two desserts, then down to the pier. This side of the street is the sweets-and-drinks side. Have charcoal-grilled toast with sangkaya custard, and hot ginger soup with rice dumplings or warm soy milk. Buy a small bag of roasted chestnuts to eat on your balcony. Walk back to the Ratchawong junction, turn left, 5 more minutes to the pier, arriving about 8:15 PM, ahead of the 8:30 PM last boat. If it is 8:15 and you are still at the stall, take the desserts to go. If the boats have stopped, see the other two ways home below.

Three ways home (important: last boat 8:30 PM)

🚤 Ferry back · ฿5 · 3 minutesThe best option. Ratchawong pier is at the bottom of Ratchawong Road where you came up. The last boat leaves at 8:30 PM. The route above gets you to the pier around 8:15, with 15 minutes to spare. Photograph the timetable sign on your way out, in case the schedule changes.
🚕 Grab or taxi · ~฿70-100 · 10 minutesAvailable on Yaowarat Road all night, over Phra Pok Klao Bridge to our door. Pin "House of Happiness Tha Din Daeng 16". If you hail a street taxi, insist on the meter.
🚶 Walk over Phra Pok Klao Bridge · free · ~25 minutesFrom the Ratchawong junction head towards the river and up onto Phra Pok Klao Bridge, which has a lit footpath the whole way and a free night view of the river. Come down on the Khlong San side and continue to Soi Tha Din Daeng 16. Good for walking off dinner.

Small tips from us

  • Not on Mondays: most Yaowarat carts take Monday off (street-cleaning day). If Monday is your only night, eat around our lane using the Tha Din Daeng guide and do Yaowarat the next evening.
  • Small cash: ferry, carts and desserts are mostly cash. Carry ฿20/50/100 notes, about ฿600 per person. Some seafood restaurants take PromptPay.
  • Leave at 6, not 8: later and you hit the longest queues of the night (8 to 9:30 PM) and miss the 8:30 PM last boat. Leaving at 6 also means you see the street just as the lights come on.
  • Vegetarian Festival (about 9 days around October): the whole street switches to vegetarian food under yellow flags and crowds double. Phadung Dao seafood stays open, but go on a weekday.
  • Better in twos or more: everything splits. Four people ordering three seafood dishes taste more and pay less each.
  • Tell us at check-in: we'll give you that season's last-boat time and mark the stops on our hand-drawn map.

Common questions

Is it safe to walk alone at night?

Yaowarat Road is busy and lit from about 6 to 11 PM, with a tourist police post near Odeon Circle. Our guests walk it alone regularly. Watch your bag in crowds, as in any big city, and avoid dark side lanes with no shops.

Can I bring children or elderly parents?

Yes, with a 4-stop version: ferry → Ratchawong → a seated dinner on Phadung Dao → desserts on the Plaeng Nam side → Grab home, skipping the walk to the gate. About 1.5 hours and 1.5 km. Pavements are narrow and stepped, so strollers are hard work.

Is there vegetarian or halal food?

Vegetarian: year-round at the jay (Chinese vegetarian) shops near Odeon Circle, and the whole street during the Vegetarian Festival; almost all desserts are meat-free. Halal is scarce on Yaowarat itself. Tell us at check-in and we'll point you to halal places within walking distance on the Khlong San side.
