Chatuchak Weekend Market — by Train from Khlong San, In at Opening, Out Before the Heat

🚆 Gold Line → Silom → Sukhumvit · ~1 hour 📅 Main market Sat–Sun ~09:00–18:00 🧭 27 sections · meet at the clock tower 💵 Small notes · polite bargaining
Illustration · Wikimedia Commons

Chatuchak is the place foreign guests ask about most after the Grand Palace, and it is the furthest from us of anything in our guides: in the north of the city, a long way from Khlong San by road. By train it is easier than it looks: the Gold Line near the hotel, two BTS lines, off at Mo Chit and through the park into the market. This page does not tell you what to buy where, because the market has thousands of stalls and they change constantly. It tells you how to arrive as it opens, how to walk it without getting lost, what to do about the heat and cash, and when to leave before the trains fill up.

Why by train, and why at opening

At weekends the roads around Chatuchak jam from mid-morning, so a car from Khlong San takes anything from half an hour to well over an hour. The train takes about an hour every time, and you never leave the BTS system on the way. The reason to arrive at opening is that most of the market is tin roofs and narrow lanes without air-conditioning: fine in the morning, hot and packed after noon. Many stalls open between about 09:00 and 10:00, so arriving then gives you roughly an hour ahead of most of the crowd.

The route — Khlong San station to the clock tower, and home before 15:00

  1. 08:15 — Gold Line, Khlong San → Krung Thon Buri (2 stops) Walk from the hotel to Khlong San station and ride 2 stops to Krung Thon Buri, then follow the signs to the BTS Silom Line platform heading into town (towards National Stadium). You stay inside the system.
  2. 08:30 — BTS Silom Line, Krung Thon Buri → Siam (6 stops), then change to the Sukhumvit Line Past Saphan Taksin and Sala Daeng to Siam, the interchange for both BTS lines. Change to the Sukhumvit Line heading towards “Khu Khot”, following the signs inside the station. No new ticket needed.
  3. 08:55 — BTS Sukhumvit Line, Siam → Mo Chit (7 stops), then walk in (~10 min) Past Victory Monument, Ari and Saphan Khwai to Mo Chit. Take the exit signposted for Chatuchak Weekend Market and follow the crowd along the edge of Chatuchak Park into the market. The other way in is MRT Kamphaeng Phet, which has an exit opening straight into the market — handy if you are arriving by MRT from somewhere else.
  4. 09:15 — The clock tower first, then pick 3–4 sections (~2 hours) The market is divided into 27 numbered sections, marked along the main walkways, with the clock tower in the middle. Go to the clock tower first, photograph the market map, and agree that anyone who gets lost comes back here. Then choose just 3 or 4 sections for what you want to buy. Do not try to cover it all — nobody manages that in a day. The main walkways are wider and shadier, the lanes inside each section narrower and hotter, so alternate between them.
  5. 11:30 — Lunch in the market, then air-conditioning (~1 hour) Eat at the food stalls along the main walkways, then get out of the midday sun in the malls next to the market, which have air-conditioning and toilets (some toilets inside the market charge a small fee). This is the moment to repack everything you have bought into one bag before a last loop.
  6. 13:30 — Home the same way, back around 14:45 Walk back to BTS Mo Chit, take the Sukhumvit Line to Siam, the Silom Line to Krung Thon Buri and the Gold Line to Khlong San. Leaving before 15:00 avoids the hour when everyone from the market boards the trains with big bags. If you have bought more than you can carry, a Grab from outside the market back to Khlong San is easier — but allow for weekend traffic.

Good to know, and our tips

  • Sun and water: the tin roofs hold the heat. Wear light clothes and shoes you can walk in for hours, carry water and top up along the way — it is sold everywhere. If you start to feel dizzy, get out to a main walkway or into one of the malls next door straight away; do not push on.
  • Cash and bargaining: many stalls take Thai QR payments, but foreign cards often will not work, so always carry small notes. Plenty of stalls have fixed prices; if you bargain, do it politely and when buying several items — smile and ask whether there is a discount, and if not, say thank you and move on.
  • Valuables and losing each other: lanes are narrow and crowded by mid-morning. Wear your bag in front and keep phones out of back pockets. Agree on the clock tower as the meeting point from the start, because in places the mobile network gets so busy that calls do not go through.
  • On weekdays it is not the Chatuchak in the photos: the main market opens only on Saturdays and Sundays; on weekdays only parts of it are open. If you are here on a weekday, Or Tor Kor market across the road, with top-quality fruit and fresh food, opens daily and is reached via MRT Kamphaeng Phet — a better plan.
  • Budget: BTS fares there and back by distance (shown on the station boards), street-food prices for lunch and water, and then whatever you buy. If you come back by Grab, add one fare, which can rise at weekends with demand.

Common questions

Train or Grab?

Going, we always suggest the train: about an hour, reliably, and you arrive as the market opens. Coming back, a Grab is easier if you have a lot of bags or a group, but the roads around the market are very slow on weekend afternoons; walking a little way from the market entrance before booking gets you a car faster.

How long should we spend in the market?

Three to four hours suits most people; if you are after something specific, two is enough. Staying longer usually means more heat rather than more shopping. If you want to linger, Chatuchak Park next door is shadier and a good place to sit before heading back.

Can we bring children or a pushchair?

Children are fine, but pushchairs are hard work in the narrow, crowded lanes inside the sections; the main walkways are manageable. With a very young child, a carrier is easier. Arriving at opening and leaving before noon suits children best: cooler and less crowded.
